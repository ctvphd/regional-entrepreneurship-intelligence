# Assignment 7 Plain-Language Policy

## Principle

Lead with the user's question or the practical meaning of the result. Give the
answer and its limits before introducing methodology.

## Technical Terminology

Retain exact statistical and research terminology in metric labels, help text,
tooltips, technical expanders, and About / Methods content. Do not replace a
technical metric with a plain phrase in a way that hides which statistic is
being shown.

## Writing Style

- Prefer short sentences, direct explanations, and concrete meaning.
- Explain what a number or chart shows and why it matters.
- Use “gap observed,” “no gap observed,” and “not available” for status.
- Describe expected startup activity as an estimate or comparison point, not
  an ideal level.
- Describe future gap risk as a historical model probability using information
  from three years earlier; never imply a current or guaranteed forecast.
- Describe coverage as data completeness, not confidence or a judgment about a
  place.
- Avoid unexplained acronyms, dense methodology paragraphs above primary
  visuals, and stigmatizing claims about communities.

## Progressive Disclosure

Plain-language labels and concise takeaways belong in the primary view. Exact
metric names, thresholds, split definitions, eligibility rules, and technical
caveats remain accessible through help text, tooltips, technical expanders,
and source-linked documentation.

## Scientific Boundary

Copy edits do not alter data, model, metric, target, threshold, denominator,
coverage rule, eligibility, or scientific conclusion. User-facing coverage
labels may clarify canonical values, but stored categories remain unchanged.
